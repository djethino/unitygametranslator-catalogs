## Why not AGPL, like the other repositories

The mod, the desktop tool, the website and the shared library are all AGPL-3.0. This one is not,
and the difference is deliberate rather than an oversight.

AGPL is a licence for **software**. What is in here is a body of **facts**: the names of languages,
their codes, which of them a provider accepts, which versions of a mod loader exist. Facts are not
ours — we did not invent that Egyptian Arabic exists or that DeepL supports thirty-odd languages,
and claiming a copyleft over them would be claiming something we do not hold.

There is one nuance, and it is why this file exists rather than a bare LICENSE. In the European
Union — and this project is maintained from France — a *compilation* can attract a **sui generis
database right** even when its individual contents are free. That right would be ours by default,
and it would leave anyone reusing this data guessing. CC0 waives it explicitly, so nobody has to
guess.

## What this means in practice

- Embedding any of these documents in a closed-source program is fine.
- Copying a table into another project is fine.
- Contributing here does not sign your work over to a licence you have to read first — which is
  the point, since a contribution is often one line adding a language somebody speaks.

## What it does not cover

The **software** that reads these documents is not covered by this licence. It lives in the other
repositories, under AGPL-3.0.

Where a document records something a third party published — the languages a provider supports,
what a model's publisher claims about it — that record is a statement of fact, kept with its
source named. It does not put those third parties' own material under CC0, and it makes no claim
about their terms.
